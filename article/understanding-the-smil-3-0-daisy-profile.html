Understanding the SMIL 3.0 DAISY Profile

The SMIL 3.0 DAISY Profile is a specialized modular specification of the Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language designed to power accessible Digital Talking Books (DTBs). This article breaks down what the profile was specifically tailored to achieve, highlighting its role in synchronizing multimedia elements, enabling granular document navigation, and standardizing accessible digital reading for individuals with print and visual disabilities.

Audio and Text Synchronization

The primary objective of the SMIL 3.0 DAISY Profile is the precise temporal alignment of synthetic or recorded human speech with digital text and visual elements. In a DAISY-compliant digital book, the profile coordinates playback so that as an audio narrator reads a sentence, the corresponding visual text on screen can be highlighted simultaneously. This multimodal presentation is crucial not only for blind and low-vision readers, but also for individuals with cognitive or learning disabilities, such as dyslexia.

Non-Linear and Hierarchical Navigation

Standard multimedia formats typically allow only sequential playback with basic forward or rewind controls. The DAISY Profile was built to support complex document hierarchies, giving users the ability to navigate a digital book just as a sighted reader navigates a printed one. It allows readers to:

  • Skip directly between chapters, sections, and subheadings.
  • Jump directly to specific page numbers corresponding to a printed edition.
  • Bypass ancillary content such as footnotes, sidebars, page numbers, and producer notes, or access them on demand.
  • Traverse text at granular levels, including paragraphs and individual sentences.

Resource Efficiency and Interoperability

SMIL 3.0 as a whole is a broad, complex standard used across diverse multimedia environments. The DAISY Profile tailors the broader SMIL 3.0 framework by selecting only the specific modules necessary for digital publishing and accessibility. By stripping away extraneous modules designed for complex web animation and visual effects, the DAISY Profile achieves:

  • Lower processing overhead, enabling low-power specialized hardware players and portable reading devices to parse and render content smoothly.
  • High interoperability among assistive technology software, mobile apps, and dedicated digital talking book players.
  • A standardized framework that later served as the architectural foundation for modern digital publishing standards, such as EPUB 3 Media Overlays.

Support for Flexible Content Models

The profile is engineered to accommodate different authoring constraints and user needs across three primary content presentations:

  1. Full Text and Full Audio: Both complete electronic text and complete audio recordings are present and synchronized.
  2. Audio with Partial Text: Designed for recorded books where text is provided primarily for structural navigation (such as table of contents and headings).
  3. Text-Only with Synthetic Speech: Designed for purely electronic text files meant to be rendered dynamically by text-to-speech (TTS) engines using predefined timing information.
