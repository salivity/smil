Understanding the SMIL Erase Attribute

In Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL), the erase attribute defines the display behavior of a media object after its scheduled playback or active rendering duration concludes. This article covers the fundamental function of the erase attribute, examines its available values, and explains how it determines whether an element disappears from the presentation space or stays visible in its final state.

The Function of the erase Attribute

When a media element (such as an image, video, or text segment) completes its active playback time in SMIL, the player must decide what to do with the rendering area the media occupied. The erase attribute dictates this post-playback behavior, giving authors control over screen persistence without requiring complex scripting.

The attribute primarily accepts two values:

  • whenDone (Default): Instructs the SMIL presentation engine to immediately remove the media object from the display canvas once its active duration ends. The area previously occupied by the object is cleared, revealing whatever content or background sits beneath it in the presentation hierarchy.
  • never: Directs the rendering engine to retain the media object in its final rendered state. A video or animation stops on its last frame and remains frozen on screen, while static media like images or text simply stay visible. The object persists until the parent container ends or another element explicitly overrides its display area.

Common Use Cases

Using erase="never" is essential in scenarios such as:

  • Slide Presentations: Keeping the final frame of an animated chart or diagram visible while an accompanying audio narration concludes.
  • Persistent Layouts: Ensuring logos, banners, or title cards remain in place after their initial transition or active animation ends.
  • Chained Media Transitions: Preventing visual flickering or blank frames between successive video or image elements by freezing the previous item until the next one loads.

Evolution in Modern SMIL Standards

While introduced in SMIL 1.0, the erase attribute was largely superseded in SMIL 2.0 and later versions by the more comprehensive fill attribute. The erase="whenDone" behavior corresponds to fill="remove", while erase="never" corresponds to fill="freeze". However, the erase attribute remains foundational for understanding timeline state management and legacy SMIL implementation.
