SMIL Audio Synchronization in DAISY

Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) serves as the timing and coordination backbone of the DAISY (Digital Accessible Information System) standard, linking written text directly to prerecorded human or synthetic audio. By structuring synchronized media elements into distinct parallel containers, SMIL enables playback engines to simultaneously render text and play matching audio at granular levels. This article explains how SMIL coordinates structural markup with audio timestamps to achieve precise paragraph-level and sentence-level synchronization, allowing dynamic highlighting and seamless navigation for accessible reading.

The Foundation: Parallel Media Elements

At its core, SMIL achieves synchronization through the <par> (parallel) container tag. A <par> element instructs the DAISY player to render two or more media assets simultaneously.

Inside a standard <par> block, SMIL pairs a reference to a visual text node with a corresponding slice of an audio file:

  • The <text> tag: Points to a specific structural element within the textual document (such as DTBook or HTML) using a URI and an XML ID fragment (e.g., src="content.html#p1").
  • The <audio> tag: Points to the target audio file and defines the exact temporal boundaries using clipBegin and clipEnd attributes (e.g., clipBegin="00:01:15.000" and clipEnd="00:01:22.500").

Paragraph-Level Synchronization

In paragraph-level synchronization, the text document marks an entire paragraph with a single identifier:

<p id="para_01">The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.</p>

The corresponding SMIL file aligns this single element to a larger segment of audio:

<par id="sync_p01">
    <text src="book.xml#para_01"/>
    <audio src="narrative.mp3" clipBegin="00:00:10.000" clipEnd="00:00:14.250"/>
</par>

When the reader navigates to this paragraph, the DAISY player highlights the entire paragraph block visually and triggers the media player to play the audio stream from the 10-second mark to the 14.25-second mark.

Sentence-Level Granularity

Achieving sentence-level granularity requires subdividing the text markup into smaller, addressable nodes. This is typically accomplished by wrapping each sentence in an <s> or <span> tag equipped with a unique ID:

<p id="para_01">
    <s id="sent_01">The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.</s>
    <s id="sent_02">It then rested beneath the shade of an oak tree.</s>
</p>

SMIL then arranges sequential <par> containers within a parent <seq> (sequence) container. Each <par> represents an individual sentence rather than the full paragraph:

<seq id="seq_para01">
    <par id="sync_s01">
        <text src="book.xml#sent_01"/>
        <audio src="narrative.mp3" clipBegin="00:00:10.000" clipEnd="00:00:14.250"/>
    </par>
    <par id="sync_s02">
        <text src="book.xml#sent_02"/>
        <audio src="narrative.mp3" clipBegin="00:00:14.250" clipEnd="00:00:18.500"/>
    </par>
</seq>

Because each sentence is isolated within its own <par> container, the player executes synchronization at milliseconds-level accuracy.

Playback and Navigation Mechanics

When executing granular synchronization, DAISY reading systems utilize the SMIL structure in two major ways:

  1. Synchronous Visual Highlighting: As playback moves from one <par> to the next, the reading system updates the Document Object Model (DOM) styling to highlight the active sentence or paragraph in real time.
  2. Granular Skipping and Seeking: Users can command the reader to skip forward or backward by sentence or paragraph. The reading system traverses the SMIL tree to find the next or previous <par> element, immediately shifting the visual focus and seeking the audio playback head to the designated clipBegin time.
