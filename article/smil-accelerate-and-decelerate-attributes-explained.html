SMIL Accelerate and Decelerate Attributes Explained

This article examines the role and functionality of the accelerate and decelerate attributes within Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) timing transitions. These attributes manipulate the playback speed of an animation across its duration, transitioning elements smoothly rather than abruptly. Below is a breakdown of what these attributes do, how they alter velocity curves, and the rules governing their implementation.

The Purpose of SMIL Easing Attributes

By default, SMIL animations progress linearly. A linear transition means an element moves, fades, or transforms at a constant velocity from start to finish, which often produces mechanical and unnatural motion.

The accelerate and decelerate attributes introduce native easing mechanisms directly into SMIL timing elements. They adjust the progression rate of the element's active duration without requiring complex keyframe definitions or cubic Bézier spline coordinates.

The accelerate Attribute

The accelerate attribute defines the proportion of the simple duration spent speeding up from a velocity of zero to the target run rate.

  • Syntax: Accepts a floating-point value between 0.0 and 1.0.
  • Default Value: 0 (no acceleration phase).
  • Behavior: Setting accelerate="0.3" causes the element to spend the first 30% of its duration ramping up from a stationary state to full speed. Once full speed is reached, the remaining 70% continues at that constant velocity (unless a deceleration value is also present). This behavior is functionally equivalent to an "ease-in" effect.

The decelerate Attribute

The decelerate attribute defines the proportion of the simple duration spent slowing down from the run rate to a final velocity of zero.

  • Syntax: Accepts a floating-point value between 0.0 and 1.0.
  • Default Value: 0 (no deceleration phase).
  • Behavior: Setting decelerate="0.5" directs the element to spend the final 50% of its duration gradually bleeding off momentum until it comes to a complete halt at the end of the timeline. This mirrors an "ease-out" effect.

Combining Both Attributes

When both accelerate and decelerate are applied simultaneously, they produce an "ease-in-out" curve:

  • Example: Setting accelerate="0.2" and decelerate="0.2" allocates the first 20% of the duration to speeding up, the middle 60% to running at peak velocity, and the final 20% to coming to a stop.
  • Pure Smooth Transitions: Setting accelerate="0.5" and decelerate="0.5" eliminates the constant-speed phase entirely; the animation continuously accelerates to a midpoint peak and immediately begins decelerating.

Constraints and Failure Handling

The SMIL specification enforces a strict mathematical rule for these attributes:

\[\text{accelerate} + \text{decelerate} \le 1.0\]

The sum of the acceleration and deceleration phases cannot exceed the total duration (represented as 1.0). If the combined total of both attributes is greater than 1.0, the browser or SMIL processor treats the configuration as an error and ignores both attributes entirely, defaulting the animation back to standard linear progression.
