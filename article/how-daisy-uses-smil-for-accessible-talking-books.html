How DAISY Uses SMIL for Accessible Talking Books

The DAISY (Digital Accessible Information System) Consortium utilizes SMIL (Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language) to bridge the gap between spoken audio and written text in digital talking books. By functioning as the timing and structural backbone, SMIL allows users with print disabilities to read, listen, and navigate complex documents seamlessly. This article explains how SMIL coordinates media playback, enables precise content navigation, and powers multi-modal accessibility in DAISY standards and modern digital publishing.

Media Synchronization

SMIL is an XML-based W3C standard designed to coordinate multiple media objects over time. In a DAISY digital talking book, the primary media components are the audio recording (human narration or synthesized speech) and the structured text file (XHTML or DTBook XML).

SMIL files act as the synchronizing engine by defining parallel media elements (<par>). Each <par> element references a specific segment of audio—defined by start and end timestamps (clipBegin and clipEnd)—alongside the corresponding text element via an XML ID. When an assistive playback device or software plays the book, SMIL ensures the spoken words align precisely with the highlighted text on the screen.

Non-Linear and Hierarchical Navigation

Traditional audio formats only allow sequential listening or basic fast-forwarding, making textbooks, reference materials, and complex literature difficult to use. DAISY solves this by mapping structural navigation directly through SMIL.

Because SMIL links directly to the underlying document structure:

  • Granular Jumping: Readers can skip between chapters, sections, subheadings, paragraphs, sentences, or even individual words without losing synchronization.
  • Skippable Structures: SMIL permits conditional playback, allowing readers to automatically bypass or dive into sidebars, footnotes, page numbers, and image descriptions.
  • Table of Contents Integration: Navigation control files (such as the DAISY NCC or NCX) point directly to SMIL clock values, allowing immediate access to any part of the publication.

Multi-Modal Accessibility

DAISY’s implementation of SMIL enables true multi-modal reading, which is essential for people with diverse visual, physical, and cognitive abilities:

  • Visual Tracking: Users with low vision, dyslexia, or learning differences can follow high-contrast or magnified text on-screen while listening to clear audio, improving comprehension.
  • Blind Readers: Users who cannot see the screen can rely purely on the synchronized audio while retaining the ability to navigate complex document hierarchies using keyboard shortcuts or screen-reader gestures.
  • Braille Displays: SMIL-backed structures allow synchronized refreshable braille output alongside audio.

The Foundation for Modern Standards

The DAISY Consortium’s use of SMIL established the model for inclusive digital publications worldwide. This architecture directly informed the development of EPUB 3 Media Overlays, which also rely on SMIL to deliver synchronized audio and highlighted text in commercial ebooks. Through SMIL, DAISY transformed digital audio from passive sound recordings into fully interactive, accessible learning environments.
