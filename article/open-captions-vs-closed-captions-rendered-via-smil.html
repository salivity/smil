Open Captions vs Closed Captions Rendered via SMIL

Understanding the difference between open captions and closed captions rendered via Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) comes down to how captions are encoded, controlled, and displayed. While open captions are permanently burned into the video stream itself and visible to all viewers, closed captions rendered through SMIL exist as separate, timed data streams synchronized via XML-based markup. This distinction impacts viewer accessibility, workflow flexibility, file size, and the ability to toggle or customize caption tracks.

What Are Open Captions?

Open captions, often called "burned-in," "baked-in," or hardcoded captions, are an inseparable part of the video frames.

  • Permanent Display: They are visible to anyone watching the video and cannot be turned off, hidden, or customized by the viewer.
  • Pixel Integration: Because the text is rendered directly into the pixels of the video file during encoding, no special player support or external caption files are required for playback.
  • Inflexible Editing: Correcting a typo, changing the font, or translating the text requires modifying the project source and re-exporting the entire video file.

What Are Closed Captions Rendered via SMIL?

SMIL is an XML-based standard designed to schedule and synchronize diverse media types—including audio, video, images, and text—within a single presentation. When closed captions are handled via SMIL:

  • Separation of Media Assets: The captions are stored in external timed-text files (such as TTML, WebVTT, or basic text) and are linked inside a SMIL playlist file using elements like <textstream> or <text>.
  • Dynamic Synchronization: SMIL uses timing attributes (such as begin, dur, and end) along with parallel (<par>) container tags to ensure the separate text stream renders in precise sync with the video.
  • Viewer Toggle: Because the text remains a distinct data layer, the end-user's media player can allow viewers to turn the captions on or off, choose between multiple language streams, or alter display styling depending on player capabilities.
  • Easy Maintenance: Updates, corrections, and additional languages can be implemented by editing the text or SMIL file directly, without needing to re-encode the original video track.

Key Differences

Feature Open Captions Closed Captions via SMIL
User Control Always visible; cannot be turned off. Can be toggled on or off by the viewer.
Delivery Method Merged into the video stream's pixel data. Delivered as separate text/XML streams linked in SMIL.
Multilingual Support Limited to one language per video export. Can reference multiple language tracks within one SMIL file.
Editing & Updates Requires complete video re-encoding. Text can be edited without altering the video asset.
Player Requirements Works on any device or standard video player. Requires a player that supports SMIL parsing and timed text.
