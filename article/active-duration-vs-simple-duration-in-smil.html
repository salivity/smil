Active Duration vs Simple Duration in SMIL

In the Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) timing model, controlling how long an element appears on a timeline involves two foundational concepts: simple duration and active duration. Simple duration defines the baseline run time of a single playback cycle for an element, while active duration represents the total time the element actually plays, taking into account repetitions, truncation, and external timing constraints. Understanding the distinction between the two is essential for accurately synchronizing complex animations, audio, and video tracks.

What Is Simple Duration?

Simple duration is the length of time an element takes to play through its content exactly once, from start to finish. It represents the intrinsic or locally defined run time of a single iteration.

  • Primary Attribute: Specified using the dur attribute (e.g., dur="5s").
  • Intrinsic Media: If dur is omitted, the simple duration defaults to the media's natural duration (such as a 30-second audio track) or to an indefinite state depending on the element type.
  • Scope: Simple duration does not account for whether the media loops, repeats, or gets cut short by outside events. It purely measures one standalone loop.

What Is Active Duration?

Active duration is the total amount of time an element remains active and visible or audible to the user. It is calculated by taking the simple duration and applying looping rules, repetition limits, and boundary constraints.

  • Primary Attributes: Influenced by repeatCount, repeatDur, min, max, and end.
  • Repetition: If an element is set to repeat, the active duration encompasses all of those repetitions.
  • Truncation: If an end event or an explicit cutoff happens before a cycle finishes, the active duration ends immediately, even if the simple duration has not completed.

Key Differences

The relationship between simple and active duration comes down to how single cycles translate into final playback.

1. Handling of Repetitions

  • Simple Duration: Remains constant regardless of repetition settings.
  • Active Duration: Multiplies or extends with repetition. For example, an element with dur="4s" and repeatCount="3" has a simple duration of 4 seconds and an active duration of 12 seconds.

2. Truncation and Premature Termination

  • Simple Duration: Defines the full potential length of a cycle and is unaffected by early stops.
  • Active Duration: Yields to early termination. If an element has dur="10s" but an event triggers an end="4s", the simple duration remains 10 seconds, but the active duration is cut to 4 seconds.

3. Boundary Constraints (min and max)

  • Simple Duration: Unaffected by min or max attributes.
  • Active Duration: Strictly bounded by them. If repeatCount="5" on a dur="2s" item creates a preliminary active duration of 10 seconds, but max="7s" is set, the final active duration is clipped to 7 seconds.

In short, simple duration defines the length of a single piece of the presentation, while active duration determines the total amount of time that piece occupies on the master timeline.
