SMIL syncBehavior: Managing Clock Drift

Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) coordinates the timed presentation of multiple media elements, such as audio, video, text, and animation. In long-running presentations, media components often diverge from their master timeline due to hardware inconsistencies, processing latency, or frame decoding issues—a phenomenon known as clock drift. The syncBehavior attribute provides authors with direct control over how a playback engine resolves these timing discrepancies, defining whether an element rigidly locks to its parent timeline or operates with tolerance for drift.

Understanding Clock Drift in SMIL

When a SMIL presentation runs, individual media renderers track time using local clocks, while the SMIL player maintains a global container timeline. Clock drift occurs when an underlying media decoder runs slightly faster or slower than the master container clock. Without intervention, audio tracks can desynchronize from video (breaking lip-sync), or subtitles can display out of step with dialogue.

How syncBehavior Operates

The syncBehavior attribute establishes the synchronization relationship between a specific media element (or container) and its parent time container. It instructs the playback engine on the degree of strictness to enforce whenever drift occurs.

The attribute accepts four primary values:

  • locked: Enforces strict synchronization. The media element is bound directly to the container clock. If drift occurs and exceeds the presentation's defined tolerance, the playback engine forces alignment. Depending on whether the element is ahead or behind, the player will pause, drop frames, or seek forward within the media to bring it back in step with the master timeline.
  • canSlip: Permits soft synchronization. The element is allowed to drift relative to the container clock without forced correction. If the media falls behind or runs ahead, the player allows it to play at its native pace without interrupting the stream with frame drops or micro-pauses. This is ideal when uninterrupted media fidelity is preferred over precise temporal alignment.
  • independent: Completely decouples the media element from the parent timeline once playback begins. The element starts according to schedule but runs on its own internal clock thereafter, ignoring container synchronization events entirely.
  • default: Inherits the behavior specified by the ancestor container's syncBehaviorDefault attribute, or reverts to the user-agent default if none is defined.

Interaction with syncTolerance

The syncBehavior attribute functions alongside syncTolerance. While syncBehavior defines how the engine responds to drift, syncTolerance defines when the engine responds.

Setting syncBehavior="locked" activates the tolerance threshold defined by syncTolerance. As long as the clock drift remains within the acceptable window (e.g., 200 milliseconds), the playback engine treats the media as synchronized. Once drift exceeds that value, the engine applies corrective measures dictated by the locked state to realign the presentation.

Practical Application

Selecting the proper syncBehavior depends on media priority:

  • Use locked for video and audio pairs requiring lip-sync, interactive quizzes, or timed captions.
  • Use canSlip for background audio tracks, ambient video loops, or scenarios where preserving smooth media playback outweighs exact frame-level coordination.
