SMIL smilText: Scrolling, Ticker, and Crawl Guide

In Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) 3.0, the <smilText> element natively manages dynamic text rendering, providing built-in mechanisms for automated scrolling, crawling tickers, and text transitions. By leveraging specific presentation attributes such as textMode, scrollRate, and integrated SMIL timing controls, authors can animate textual content smoothly across display regions without relying on external scripts or video pre-rendering.

The Role of the textMode Attribute

The primary mechanism governing automated movement in <smilText> is the textMode attribute. It defines how text enters, moves across, and exits the assigned rendering area (region). The two fundamental values for motion are:

  • scroll (Vertical Motion): Moves text vertically through the defined region, typically from bottom to top. This behavior is standard for movie credits, teleprompters, or long-form announcements that exceed the visible bounding box.
  • crawl (Horizontal Motion): Moves text horizontally across the display region, usually from right to left (depending on the text writing direction). This creates standard continuous ticker tapes or breaking news banners.

In addition to scroll and crawl, the attribute supports static modes like replace (where text swaps out instantly) and append (where new text blocks appear incrementally without scrolling).

Controlling Velocity and Pacing

The speed and duration of ticker and crawl movements can be defined either implicitly through standard SMIL timing or explicitly via motion attributes:

  1. Duration-Based Pacing (dur): When you specify an overall duration (e.g., dur="10s") on the <smilText> element without a fixed speed, the SMIL user agent automatically calculates the movement velocity. The text enters from off-screen and completes its passage fully out of view within the allocated timeframe.
  2. Rate-Based Pacing (scrollRate): The scrollRate attribute specifies a fixed movement speed, typically defined in pixels per second. When scrollRate is used, the total duration can be determined dynamically based on the length of the string and the dimensions of the layout region.

Directionality and Layout Flow

Text direction in automated scrolling and crawling is closely tied to the textWritingMode attribute:

  • In standard left-to-right languages (like English), setting textMode="crawl" defaults the horizontal transit from the right edge of the container to the left.
  • Setting textMode="scroll" moves text against the block progression direction, normally scrolling upward from the bottom boundary.
  • For bidirectional or right-to-left scripts (like Arabic or Hebrew), the crawl direction automatically inverses to preserve readability.

Basic Syntax Example

Below is an example demonstrating both a horizontal news ticker (crawl) and vertical credits (scroll):

<smil xmlns="http://www.w3.org/ns/SMIL" version="3.0" baseProfile="Language">
  <head>
    <layout>
      <root-layout width="800" height="600" backgroundColor="black" />
      <!-- Region for Horizontal Ticker -->
      <region id="tickerRegion" bottom="0" left="0" width="800" height="40" backgroundColor="#111" />
      <!-- Region for Vertical Credits -->
      <region id="creditsRegion" top="100" left="200" width="400" height="300" />
    </layout>
  </head>
  <body>
    <par>
      <!-- Horizontal Ticker Crawl -->
      <smilText region="tickerRegion" 
                textMode="crawl" 
                scrollRate="50px" 
                textColor="yellow" 
                textFontSize="20px">
        Breaking News: SMIL 3.0 natively handles dynamic tickers and scrolling text without external scripting.
      </smilText>

      <!-- Vertical Scrolling Credits -->
      <smilText region="creditsRegion" 
                textMode="scroll" 
                dur="15s" 
                textColor="white" 
                textFontSize="16px" 
                textAlign="center">
        Directed by Media Engine
        Produced by Web Standards
        Animated with smilText
      </smilText>
    </par>
  </body>
</smil>

In-line Synchronization with <clear /> and <tev>

Within a scrolling or crawling <smilText> block, developers can introduce timed event markers (<tev>) to trigger secondary multimedia events (such as sound effects or image changes) exactly when a specific word passes into view. Furthermore, the <clear /> element can be inserted into the stream to flush previous content dynamically during ongoing operations.
