Audio Codecs for 3GPP SMIL Mobile Standards

During the development of early mobile multimedia standards, Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) was adopted by the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) to orchestrate layout, timing, and synchronization in Multimedia Messaging Service (MMS) and Packet-switched Streaming Service (PSS). Because early cellular networks faced strict bandwidth and processing constraints, 3GPP standardized a distinct set of audio codecs to pair with SMIL presentations, primarily categorized into voice-optimized speech codecs, general-purpose audio codecs, and synthetic audio formats.

Speech Codecs: AMR-NB and AMR-WB

The core speech codec designated for 3GPP SMIL deployments was AMR-NB (Adaptive Multi-Rate Narrowband). Standardized in 3GPP TS 26.140 for MMS and TS 26.234 for streaming, AMR-NB was the mandatory default speech codec for mobile terminals. Operating across bitrates from 4.75 kbps to 12.2 kbps, it compressed voice recordings into minimal file sizes suitable for 2G and 2.5G data channels.

As networks evolved, 3GPP added support for AMR-WB (Adaptive Multi-Rate Wideband), often standardized as ITU-T G.722.2. AMR-WB provided significantly richer voice quality by increasing the audio frequency band from 300–3400 Hz to 50–7000 Hz, making it suitable for higher-tier devices and richer multimedia presentations.

General Audio Codecs: AAC and HE-AAC

For music and complex acoustic tracks that speech codecs could not faithfully replicate, 3GPP paired SMIL with MPEG-4 based audio codecs:

  • MPEG-4 AAC LC (Low Complexity): Served as the primary high-fidelity stereo audio solution for 3GPP multimedia containers (.3gp). It delivered acceptable music quality at bitrates typically starting around 64 kbps.
  • HE-AAC (High-Efficiency AAC / aacPlus v1): Introduced to drastically lower bandwidth usage for streaming and MMS by combining AAC-LC with Spectral Band Replication (SBR), delivering usable audio quality at bitrates between 24 and 48 kbps.
  • HE-AAC v2 (Enhanced aacPlus): Later specified in 3GPP Release 6, this format added Parametric Stereo (PS) to SBR, allowing full-frequency stereo playback at bitrates as low as 16 to 24 kbps, optimizing playback within lightweight SMIL presentations.

Synthetic Audio: SP-MIDI and Mobile XMF

To achieve background music and sound effects with virtually zero bandwidth consumption, 3GPP standards integrated synthetic audio codecs alongside SMIL:

  • SP-MIDI (Scalable Polyphony MIDI): Mandatory in early MMS profiles, SP-MIDI transmitted note and instrument commands rather than raw waveforms, allowing devices to synthesize complex polyphonic sounds locally with files measuring only a few kilobytes.
  • Mobile DLS and Mobile XMF: Permitted devices to use custom downloadable soundbanks, ensuring uniform ringtones and soundtrack playback across hardware from different handset manufacturers.
