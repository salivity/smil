Continuous Background Audio in SMIL Presentations

This article explains how Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) enables continuous background audio playback while cycling through multiple visual slides. By combining SMIL's parallel (<par>) and sequential (<seq>) temporal containers, authors can decouple audio playback from individual slide lifecycles, ensuring an uninterrupted soundtrack across an entire presentation.

In SMIL, managing media timing relies on structuring temporal containers. When a presentation requires audio to persist across multiple visual transitions, the audio track and the sequence of slides must run concurrently in a parent parallel container.

The Structural Pattern

To achieve continuous background audio, place the <audio> element and a <seq> container as sibling elements inside a top-level <par> element:

<smil xmlns="http://www.w3.org/ns/SMIL">
  <body>
    <par>
      <!-- Background Audio playing continuously -->
      <audio src="background-music.mp3" dur="60s" />

      <!-- Visual slides advancing in sequence -->
      <seq>
        <img src="slide1.jpg" dur="15s" />
        <img src="slide2.jpg" dur="15s" />
        <img src="slide3.jpg" dur="15s" />
        <img src="slide4.jpg" dur="15s" />
      </seq>
    </par>
  </body>
</smil>

Key Components

  • The <par> Container: Instructs the player to start both the background audio and the visual timeline simultaneously at the beginning of the presentation.
  • The <seq> Container: Groups visual elements (such as <img>, <video>, or text) and displays them one after another in order. Each element dictates its own display duration using the dur attribute without interrupting sibling tracks in the outer <par>.
  • The <audio> Element: Operates independently of the nested <seq>. Its duration can match the total presentation time, or it can use attributes like repeatCount="indefinite" to loop continuously until the visual sequence concludes.

Synchronization Control

To handle mismatches between the duration of the audio and the visual slides, SMIL provides the endsync attribute on the parent <par> element:

  • endsync="first" terminates the entire presentation as soon as either the audio or the slide sequence finishes.
  • endsync="last" ensures playback continues until all child tracks—both the audio and the complete sequence of slides—have ended.
  • endsync="id(slideSequence)" forces the entire presentation, including the audio, to cut off precisely when the specified slide sequence finishes.
