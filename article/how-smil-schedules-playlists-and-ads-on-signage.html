How SMIL Schedules Playlists and Ads on Signage

Synchronized Multimedia Integration Language (SMIL) schedules repeating playlists and commercial ad insertions by using structured XML markup to control timing, layering, and media sequence. Through basic temporal containers, repetition attributes, and wall-clock scheduling rules, SMIL enables digital signage media players to continuously loop content while dynamically interrupting or overlaying targeted advertisements at precise intervals.

The Foundation: Sequential and Parallel Containers

SMIL organizes media using two primary timing containers: <seq> (sequential) and <par> (parallel).

  • <seq>: Plays child elements one after another in order. This serves as the foundation for traditional signage playlists, where videos, graphics, and animations are queued sequentially.
  • <par>: Plays all child elements simultaneously. Digital signage uses <par> to segment a screen into multiple zones—such as running a background video, an RSS ticker, and a logo area at the same time.

Creating Repeating Playlists

To make a standard playlist repeat indefinitely, SMIL applies the repeatCount attribute to the container holding the media loop.

<seq repeatCount="indefinite">
    <video src="promo1.mp4" />
    <img src="graphic1.jpg" dur="10s" />
    <video src="promo2.mp4" />
</seq>

In this configuration, once promo2.mp4 completes, the media player automatically cycles back to promo1.mp4. The dur attribute specifies how long static assets, such as images or web pages, remain visible before moving to the next item.

Wall-Clock Ad Scheduling

Commercial ad insertions often rely on specific calendar dates and times rather than continuous loops. SMIL uses the wallclock() function to trigger ad campaigns at designated moments.

<seq>
    <video src="sponsor_ad.mp4" begin="wallclock(R/2026-03-30T14:00:00/PT1H)" />
</seq>

By defining a recurrence rule with R, an ad can be scheduled to trigger at regular real-world intervals, such as at the top of every hour (PT1H), regardless of where the standard playlist is currently playing.

Handling Ad Insertions and Preemption with <excl>

The exclusive container (<excl>) manages conflicts between a background loop and a high-priority commercial insertion. In an <excl> block, only one child element can play at a time, allowing high-priority events to pause or stop lower-priority playback.

  1. Preemption: When an ad's start condition is met (via wall-clock time, external API call, or sensor input), the player halts the primary playlist.
  2. Execution: The commercial ad plays in full.
  3. Resumption: Using SMIL preemption rules (such as pauseDisplay), the primary playlist either resumes from the exact frame where it stopped or skips to the next scheduled item.

Zone-Based Secondary Ad Insertions

Rather than interrupting the main content entirely, SMIL also supports non-intrusive ad insertion through screen zoning. Within a master <par> layout:

  • Zone A runs the main looping content continuously via <seq repeatCount="indefinite">.
  • Zone B stays hidden or runs ambient branding until an ad trigger activates a commercial banner or video overlay for a specified duration (dur="15s").

Once the advertisement concludes, Zone B clears or reverts to its idle state while Zone A continues uninterrupted, ensuring zero black frames or playback stutter.
